# Clustering, Constraint Groups and Dispatch-Down on Ireland's High-Voltage Grid: A Sourced Briefing for the "Efficiency in Ireland's Electricity Grid" Hackathon

## TL;DR
- **Yes — EirGrid/SONI genuinely use a form of "clustering," and it has a specific published name: "constraint groups" in the Wind Dispatch Tool (WDT).** Wind/solar farms are grouped by their *electrical effectiveness* at relieving a given transmission overload; the public *Wind Dispatch Tool Constraint Group Overview* (latest edition dated 1 February 2024, 74 pages) lists ~39 named groups across seven regional categories. The mapping is published at the **transmission-station/node level**, but the **individual named-farm-to-group mapping is NOT published** — this is a confirmed public-data gap.
- **The user's headline figures are largely correct but need date/scope precision.** Verified: all-island renewables = 40.0% of demand in 2024; 13,288 GWh wind generated all-island in 2024; RoI wind dispatch-down = 11.3% in 2025. The "€567m" figure is specifically the **Network Imperfections Charge *allowed* for tariff year 2024/25 (€567.21m)**, and the 2025/26 forecast is higher and contested. The "80% of curtailment from MUON" claim (arXiv:2302.07143) is verified but applies to **curtailment specifically (2020–21), not total dispatch-down.**
- **The single biggest efficiency lever is not the SNSP cap but the minimum-conventional-units and local-network constraints.** In 2024 EirGrid attributed 96% of RoI *curtailment* to the requirement to keep a minimum number of conventional units online (MUON); in 2025 transmission *constraints* (6.6%) overtook curtailment (4.7%) as the larger cause of RoI wind dispatch-down. Interconnector delays (Celtic → Q4 2028; North–South → October 2031) mean local constraints will persist.

---

## Key Findings

1. **"Clustering" = constraint groups.** The mechanism the organiser referred to is real, documented and central to how curtailment/constraint is applied. It is an effectiveness/sensitivity-based grouping, not a spectral-clustering or graph-partitioning algorithm.
2. **Two distinct dispatch-down types with different compensation.** *Curtailment* (system-wide: SNSP, MUON, inertia, RoCoF) is applied all-island pro-rata; *constraint* (local network) is applied to specific groups. The distinction was approved in SEM-13-011 and matters for who gets paid.
3. **The market schedule and the physical dispatch are separate.** SEMO runs the I-SEM markets; EirGrid/SONI redispatch in real time for security. Dispatch-down is a redispatch (balancing) action, and its cost is recovered from suppliers via the Imperfections Charge.
4. **SNSP is at 75% and has been permanent since ~2022; it is usually NOT the binding constraint.** MUON dominates curtailment.
5. **The most-constrained regions are the North-West (Donegal), West (Mayo/Galway/Connacht) and South-West (Kerry/Cork)** — exactly where the most constraint groups exist.
6. **Good open tooling exists** — PyPSA (used for Irish research), the OATS toolbox (Strathclyde), and a brand-new (25 Aug 2026) arXiv paper + GitHub repo that builds an OATS-based MILP model of the Irish system specifically to estimate curtailment and constraint.

## Details

### Section 1 (CORE) — Constraint groups in the Wind Dispatch Tool: the "clustering"

**Verified.** The primary document is *"Wind Dispatch Tool Constraint Group Overview."* The current public edition is dated **1 February 2024** and runs to **74 pages**; earlier editions are dated 28 February 2020 (28 pages) and 30 September 2022. It is published on the EirGrid site and mirrored by SEMO (sem-o.com).

- URLs: https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Wind-Dispatch-Tool-Constraint-Group-Overview_0.pdf ; overview page https://cms.eirgrid.ie/wind-dispatch-tool-constraint-group-overview-0 ; 2020 mirror https://www.sem-o.com/sites/semo/files/documents/wind-dispatch-tool-constraint-groups.pdf ; landing page https://www.eirgrid.ie/grid/system-and-renewable-data-reports

**What a constraint group is (verbatim from the document):** *"In order to manage these constraints, wind/solar farms are grouped together depending on their effectiveness to alleviate constraints. The effectiveness is a measure of the change in wind/solar farm output relative to the change in the level of the 'base case', 'N-1' or 'N-1-1' overload. The effectiveness of each wind/solar farm is a function of the topology of the transmission network."* Farms connected at the same transmission station generally have the same effectiveness and are grouped together; farms that don't substantially help relieve a given overload are excluded.

**How membership is determined (verbatim):** *"For each constraint group, all wind/solar farms with Controllability Category 2 connected to the transmission stations in that group (either connected directly to the transmission station or connected via the local distribution network) are included in the predefined constraint groups in the WDT."* Groups are pre-defined for fast real-time application; the operator selects a group and enters an MW reduction, and the WDT computes and issues an individual MW setpoint to each farm.

**How many groups and their names (from the 1 February 2024 contents, verified):** ~39 named groups across **seven regional categories**:
- **Northern Ireland (5):** 1 NW Northern; 2 NW Southern; 3 Oma-Dromore; 4 All NI; 5 Kells–Rasharkin.
- **Ireland North West (7, incl. one "Retired"):** e.g. Letterkenny A1/A2 busbar flows; Sligo–Flagford; Carrick-on-Shannon–Arva; Sliabh Bawn–Lanesboro; Binbane–Cathaleen's Fall; Corderry–Srananagh (Retired).
- **Ireland West (9):** e.g. Lanesboro–Mullingar; Shannonbridge–Maynooth; Galway/Salthill/Cashla; Cauteen voltage stability; Lisheen–Thurles; Castlebar–Cloon; Moy–Glenree; Cunghill–Sligo; Thurles instability.
- **Ireland South West (11, incl. one "Retired," several "Outage Driven"):** e.g. Moneypoint transformer (Retired); Rathkeale–Limerick; Glenlara–Ballynahulla/Charleville; Charleville–Mallow; Ballylickey voltage stability; Dunmanway–Bandon; Knockanure–Kilpaddoge; Clashavoon–Macroom; Clahane–Tralee; Cauteen–Killonan.
- **Ireland South East (4, all "Outage Driven"):** Arklow–Ballybeg (×2); Thornsberry–Derryiron; Crane–Wexford.
- **Ireland North East (2):** Ratrussan–Louth; Platin–Drybridge.
- **Ireland Nationwide (1):** "All IE."

**What the groups cover:** each group is tied to a specific overload/voltage-stability risk on named 110 kV / 220 kV / 275 kV circuits, and lists the **transmission stations/nodes** (not the individual farm names) whose connected controllable renewables belong to the group. Groups are modified/created/retired as the network topology and outages change.

**CONFIRMED GAP — farm-to-group mapping:** The public document identifies **transmission stations/nodes** per group, and states membership = "all Controllability Category 2 wind/solar farms at those stations." It does **not** publish an explicit list of named wind farms per group. The named-farm-level mapping therefore is **not publicly documented** in this report (one can approximate it by cross-referencing station locations, but EirGrid does not publish the definitive list). The 2026 Strathclyde/RES modelling paper (arXiv:2608.24464, below) confirms the added complication that in Ireland "generators can belong to **multiple, and potentially overlapping, pro-rata groups**."

**Corroboration from industry:** The consultancy TNEI markets an "On-Demand Dispatch Down Report" and states its methodology "follows EirGrid's Wind Dispatch Tool Constraint Group Overview," confirming external practitioners treat this document as the authoritative description.

### Section 2 (CORE) — The actual dispatch and scheduling machinery

**Verified.** Ireland/NI run a single all-island market, the **Integrated Single Electricity Market (I-SEM)**, live since **1 October 2018**. Market operation is split: **SEMO** operates the markets (day-ahead via NEMOs, intraday, and the Balancing Market) and does settlement; **EirGrid and SONI** (the TSOs) do system balancing and real-time dispatch.

- **Market vs operational dispatch:** The day-ahead/intraday markets produce an economic (essentially unconstrained/"copper-plate") schedule. The TSOs then **redispatch** in real time to respect physical security limits. Participants submit **Physical Notifications (PNs)**; after **Balancing Market gate closure** the TSO issues **dispatch instructions** using **inc/dec offers** (complex offers before gate closure, simple offers after). Source: SEMO "Balancing Market Principles Statement" v9.0; SEMO "Industry Guide to the I-SEM Markets" (Chapter 4); Business Process BP_SO_3.2 "Issue Dispatch Instructions."
- **Scheduling/pricing software:** Historically the SEM used **MSP (Market Scheduling and Pricing)** software to compute least-cost dispatch and System Marginal Price; under I-SEM the scheduling-and-dispatch tooling performs long- and short-term scheduling (unit commitment/de-commitment advice) that feeds real-time dispatch. EirGrid also uses the commercial **PLEXOS** platform for studies (its exact formulation is not public, per arXiv:2608.24464).
- **EMS/SCADA:** Real-time monitoring of power flows and voltages and contingency modelling determine constraint location/magnitude; the WDT issues MW setpoints to each farm's control system. (The constraint document describes this control loop but does not name the specific EMS vendor — treat EMS/SCADA vendor detail as **not publicly documented** here.)
- **Priority dispatch:** Wind/solar are priority dispatch per the EirGrid/SONI licences and **SEM-11-062**; the constraint-vs-curtailment distinction was approved in **SEM-13-011**. Dispatch-down of renewables is done per **controllability category** (Grid Code) and then **pro-rata within a group**.
- **Tie-breaker / pro-rata rule:** When several farms sit in the same constraint group, the reduction is shared pro-rata (setpoints computed per the WDT's effectiveness weighting); a unit can be affected by several groups in parallel and receives the highest binding setpoint. The 2024 SEM consultation **SEM-24-044** ("TSOs' proposed definitions of Curtailment, Constraint and Energy Balancing") describes this rebalancing/energy-balancing interaction and the move (under the EU Clean Energy Package, Arts. 12/13) toward **Non-Priority Dispatch Renewables (NPDRs)** and pro-rata redispatch for all variable renewables.
- **Dispatch Balancing Costs (DBC):** Payments arising because generators are instructed differently from the market schedule (constraint/curtailment redispatch, make-whole payments, net imbalance energy cost). These are recovered from suppliers via the **Imperfections Charge** (a.k.a. Network Imperfections Charge). Source: EirGrid "Operational Constraints / Customer Information"; SEM Committee imperfections decisions (below).

### Section 3 — Physical/operational limits driving dispatch-down

**SNSP (System Non-Synchronous Penetration) — verified:** the real-time ratio of non-synchronous generation (wind, solar, HVDC imports, incl. BESS discharge) to demand (plus HVDC exports/BESS charging). History of increases (verified from EirGrid annual reports and the Operational Policy Roadmap): **50% (2015) → 65% → 70% → 75%.** The 75% **trial started 15 April 2021** and ran until **31 March 2022**, after which *"The power system is now permanently operated to an SNSP Limit of 75%."* The 2024 annual report confirms **75% applied for the full year 2024**, and there is no public evidence of an increase beyond 75% as of 2026 — EirGrid states it is "working toward" 80% next. The **2030 ambition is up to 95%** (Operational Policy Roadmap 2022–2023, Dec 2022 roadmap to 2030). SNSP is *"a system security metric… established from the results of the DS3 programme."*

**MUON (Minimum Number of Units Online) — verified:** historically **8 large conventional units (5 in Ireland + 3 in NI)**. A **7-set trial** (4 IE + 3 NI) commenced **30 May 2023**; the 2024 report notes a status change on **7 April 2024** to the 7-set configuration. MUON is a "catch-all" stability constraint (frequency, voltage, system strength).

**Inertia / RoCoF / reserve — verified (2024 report):** minimum synchronous **inertia floor of 23,000 MWs** applied for the full year; **RoCoF limit ±1.0 Hz/s**; a requirement for **50 MW of negative reserve** on conventional generators in NI.

**Verifying arXiv:2302.07143 (Hurtado, Kërçi, Tweed, Kennedy, Kamaluddin, Milano; UCD; 2023, IEEE):** **VERIFIED, with a scope caveat.** The paper states that the main driver of **curtailment** in the AIPS during **2020–2021** was **MUON (≈80% of the time)**, with the **SNSP limit accounting for <20%**, and RoCoF/inertia negligible. This is correct as stated — but note it is about **curtailment reason codes specifically** (system-wide dispatch-down), NOT total dispatch-down (which also includes local constraint). The finding has strengthened since: per EirGrid data cited by the Irish grid-literacy resource climatejargonbuster.ie, *"In 2024, EirGrid reported that 96% of curtailment in the Republic was caused by this minimum conventional generation requirement,"* with eight large synchronous units (five in Ireland, three in NI) required to run. The same source reports the **SNSP** limit "was responsible for only about 1% of all dispatch-down across the island in 2024… That share rose to 17% in 2025, suggesting the SNSP ceiling is becoming a more significant constraint as renewable capacity grows." So the arXiv claim is accurate and the dominance of MUON persists — though SNSP is rising in importance.

**DS3 and successors — verified:** The **DS3 programme** ("Delivering a Secure Sustainable Electricity System") delivered the operational trials and a suite of ~14 system-services products (procured via six-monthly "gate" tenders; Regulated Arrangements effective 1 May 2018). It is being replaced by **FASS (Future Arrangements for System Services)**, whose centrepiece is a **Day-Ahead System Services Auction (DASSA)**, expected to go live **May 2027** (long-stop 30 September 2027); DS3 Regulated Arrangements were extended to bridge "the Gap." A **Low Carbon Inertia Services (LCIS)** procurement concluded in 2024, with inertia delivery expected during 2027 and a second LCIS phase (~2030) under consultation (July 2025). Sources: EirGrid DS3 page; FASS consultation/recommendation papers (SEM-23-103; SONI/EirGrid FASS PIRs); Lexology/Mason Hayes Curran legal summaries.

### Section 4 — Network topology and thermal constraints

**Verified.** EirGrid plans/operates the grid at **110 kV, 220 kV, 275 kV and 400 kV** across the island (275 kV is predominantly the Northern Ireland backbone). Live topology is on the EirGrid **Smart Grid Dashboard** (transmission map at smartgriddashboard.com), which layers 400/275/220/110 kV lines, cables, HVDC and stations. (Exact counts of substations/circuits are best taken live from the Dashboard or the Transmission Forecast Statement rather than a fixed figure here — treat any single national count as edition-dependent.)

**Interconnectors:**
- **East–West Interconnector (EWIC):** 500 MW HVDC (±200 kV), Shotton (N. Wales) ↔ Rush North Beach (Co. Dublin), 261 km, commissioned **2012**, EirGrid-owned.
- **Moyle Interconnector:** links Northern Ireland to Scotland (HVDC); active during NI north–south constraint events (per Utility Regulator NI analysis). (Precise MW rating not pinned to a primary source here.)
- **Celtic Interconnector:** 700 MW HVDC (±320 kV), 575 km, Knockraha (Co. Cork) ↔ La Martyre (Brittany). **NOT yet commissioned.** Per EirGrid's letter to TD Malcolm Byrne (reported by the Irish Examiner, 2026), *"the new commissioning date for the project is now expected for Q4 2028… Adverse weather conditions could impact this date"* — a slip from earlier targets of 2026 → 2027 → spring 2028. Cost is now estimated at up to ~€2bn versus the €1.623bn announced in 2022 (Irish Times, 8 Jun 2026); ~€530.7m CEF/EU support (2019). It will be Ireland's first direct link to continental Europe.
- **North–South Interconnector:** proposed **400 kV** overhead line, ~138 km, Turleenan (Co. Tyrone) ↔ Woodland (Co. Meath), ~407 pylons. Has full planning/legal consent in both jurisdictions but **construction has not started in RoI** as of mid-2026. Completion is estimated for **October 2031** — per an EirGrid spokesperson quoted in the *Irish Times* (16 Feb 2025, "North-south electricity interconnector delayed by a further three years until October 2031") and reconfirmed in the *Meath Chronicle* (17 Jul 2026). Designated a critical-infrastructure project in 2026. This single north–south bottleneck is a major driver of NI wind constraint.

**Most-constrained regions — verified:** the **North-West (Donegal**: Letterkenny/Binbane), the **West (Mayo, Galway, Connacht seaboard)** and the **South-West (Kerry/Cork)** — precisely the areas with the most constraint groups (SW 11, West 9, NW 7). These are high-wind, weak-network areas far from Dublin demand. Grid remedies underway include uprating ~2,300 km of 110/220 kV lines and the 60 km, 110 kV North Connacht project (CP0816, Moy↔Tonroe — the country's first long underground cable circuit).

**Dynamic Line Rating (DLR) — verified:** Ireland does use/trial DLR. **Heimdall Power** announced a multi-year agreement with **ESB Networks and EirGrid** — including a radial-line pilot on the **Lisheen Wind Farm** connection "designed to allow expansion of the wind farm's capacity by close to 30%" — and separately won a competitive tender with **NIE Networks**; both aim to "unlock up to 30% more capacity on existing circuits, without physically upgrading the lines." The roadmap allocates ~€19m to power-flow control and DLR. Line design in Ireland is CIGRE-based (CIGRE TB.601); EirGrid uses seasonal static ratings (winter/spring-autumn/summer). So DLR is deployed selectively, not network-wide.

### Section 6 — Genuine use of clustering / grouping / partitioning

- **Constraint groups (Section 1) are the main clustering** and are effectiveness/sensitivity-based (essentially power-transfer-distribution-factor screening), **not** spectral clustering or graph partitioning. **No public EirGrid document describes use of named graph-clustering algorithms (spectral clustering, community detection, etc.).** State as not documented.
- **Transmission Constraint Groups (TCGs) in scheduling:** EirGrid's PR5 Imperfections & Constraints Multi-Year Plan (2025–2029) states physical network limits are reflected in the least-cost market schedule via **TCGs**; the TSOs plan to "develop operational constraints to target local, regional and system constraints," including a new **system-strength policy, a regional inertia framework**, and eventual relaxation/removal of the SNSP and MUON constraints (kept only as reportable metrics). In 2025 the TSO planned a position paper on **regional inertia floors**, with a possible 2026 trial.
- **Market design (zonal vs nodal):** the SEM is a **single all-island zonal market** — there is **no nodal/locational marginal pricing** in Ireland. SOEF consultation feedback explicitly called for "locational signals, congestion products and long-duration storage," but no nodal pricing has been adopted. This is a live efficiency debate and a legitimate hackathon angle.
- **Shaping Our Electricity Future (SOEF):** EirGrid/SONI's roadmap to carry 80% renewables by 2030, launched October 2021 (v1.1, July 2023). It evaluated four network-development approaches (Generation-led, Developer-led, Technology-led, Demand-led) and introduced **"renewable hubs"** — *"where renewable projects in a geographic area are gathered into a new transmission sub-station"* — a real geographic/clustering concept in planning.
- **ECP / connection batching:** The CRU's **Enduring Connection Policy** processed connection applications in annual "batches" (ECP-1 2018; ECP-2.1–2.4 2020–2023). The successor **ECP-GSS** (CRU/2024101, Sept 2024) moved to **bi-annual batches** (deadlines end-March and end-September; first ECP-GSS batch 30 Sept 2025; ECP-GSS-3 batch 30 Sept 2026), removed caps for renewables, and supports RED III repowering and renewable hubs. This is "clustering" of applications in time, not electrical clustering.

### Section 7 — Tooling and data

- **PyPSA / PyPSA-Eur:** open-source (Brown, Hörsch, Schlachtberger 2018). **Dublin City University and the SFI Insight Centre for Data Analytics use PyPSA** for the OESM project modelling the Irish energy system (per PyPSA's official users list). PyPSA-Eur (arXiv:1806.01613) covers the full European transmission system including Ireland; it is graph-based (nodes = buses, edges = lines) and openly licensed.
- **OATS (Optimisation and Analysis Toolbox for power Systems):** University of Strathclyde (Bukhsh, Edmunds, Bell, *IEEE Trans. Power Systems* 35(5):3552–3561, 2020, doi:10.1109/TPWRS.2020.2986081). Python/Pyomo; **GNU GPL v3** (copyleft). Repo: github.com/bukhsh/oats.
- **arXiv:2608.24464 — VERIFIED to exist and matches the user's description.** *"Modelling Renewable Curtailment and Constraints in Ireland's Electricity System,"* **submitted 25 August 2026** (v1, classified [math.OC]). Authors: **Richard Nayer, Sam Hodges, Waqquas Bukhsh (University of Strathclyde)** and **Claver Chitambo, Chanura Wijeratne (Renewable Energy Solutions / RES)** — note the author list differs slightly from the user's recollection ("Nayer, Hodges, Bukhsh, Chitambo, Wijeratne"). Abstract (verbatim): *"This paper describes the electricity markets and operational processes in the Irish power system and translates them into a Mixed Integer Linear Programming (MILP) model. The model is designed to estimate renewable generation Curtailment and Constraint. A full mathematical formulation is presented and tested on both a simple example and a realistic model of the Irish transmission network."* It **extends OATS** in a three-stage pipeline: **(1) Market** — copper-plate unit commitment as surrogate for the ex-ante market position; **(2) Curtailed** — add SNSP and MUON constraints to compute system-wide curtailment; **(3) Constrained** — reformulate as unit-commitment DC-OPF to capture local transmission constraints and further redispatch. Only SNSP and MUON are modelled (RoCoF/inertia ≈0.6% of curtailment, neglected). The realistic Irish network model has **446 buses, 586 lines, 183 transformers, 288 generators**; tested on **July 2025 at half-hourly resolution (1,488 periods)**, solving in ~32 minutes on a laptop. **Key result:** it reasonably tracks wind/solar output but **underestimates total dispatch-down, constraints and MUON, while overestimating SNSP curtailment** (attributed to lack of N-1 security, an unvalidated network model, and no time-coupling). Data sources: **All-Island Ten-Year Transmission Forecast Statement 2022, SEMO market data, EirGrid System and Renewable Data Reports, Elexon interconnector flows**, and the SEMO "Weekly Operational Constraints Report" for constraint definitions. It confirms Irish farms belong to "multiple, potentially overlapping pro-rata groups." (Note: its numeric modelled-vs-actual validation totals are presented in Figures 7–8 rather than as extractable text — read the figures directly.)
- **GitHub github.com/richardnayer/oats_curtailment — VERIFIED to exist.** Public, ~58 commits, tagged as "release for PSCC paper." Contains **`ireland_case_*.xlsx`** input workbooks: `ireland_case_03032025-1030.xlsx`, `ireland_case_05072025.xlsx`, `ireland_case_july2025.xlsx`, `ireland_case_test.xlsx`, plus `illustrative_testcase.xlsx`, `end-to-end-testcase.xlsx`, `results.xlsx`, and `pyomo_models`, `data_io`, `tests` folders and `requirements.txt`. These OATS-format workbooks hold the network/case data (buses, lines, transformers, generators and security-constraint definitions). **LICENCE: could NOT be confirmed** — the repo navigation showed only a README (title only, no substantive description); a LICENSE file was not visible. Since it derives from OATS (GPL v3), GPL v3 obligations would apply, but **treat the repo's own licence as unconfirmed** and check directly before reuse. The `.xlsx` internal sheet structure could not be inspected via web tools. The paper also cites a separate R. Nayer (2025) "Ireland electricity system model" repo, which could not be independently located/confirmed.
- **EirGrid's own public data:** the **Smart Grid Dashboard** (live all-island generation/demand/fuel-mix and transmission map); **System and Renewable Data Reports** (monthly aggregate + individual controllable farm constraint/curtailment reports with reason codes and time-stamped dispatch instructions); the **(Ten-Year) Transmission Forecast Statement**; and the monthly **Operational Constraints Report**. ## Recommendations

**For framing the hackathon project (do this first):**
1. **Anchor the problem on the right constraint.** Optimise against **MUON/minimum-conventional-units and local network constraints**, not SNSP — the data show MUON drove ~80% of curtailment (2020–21) and 96% of RoI curtailment (2024), and constraints (6.6%) now exceed curtailment (4.7%) in RoI (2025). A project that only relaxes SNSP addresses <20% of the problem.
2. **Adopt the constraint-group abstraction directly.** Model dispatch-down as (a) all-island pro-rata curtailment + (b) per-group local constraint, mirroring the WDT. This is defensible and matches EirGrid's own method.

**For tooling (staged):**
3. **Start from the Nayer/Bukhsh OATS model (arXiv:2608.24464 + github.com/richardnayer/oats_curtailment).** It is purpose-built for exactly this question, uses only public data, and its `ireland_case_july2025.xlsx` gives you a ready 446-bus / 288-generator Irish case. **Verify the repo licence before redistributing** (assume GPL v3 constraints).
4. **If you need investment/capacity-expansion angles, use PyPSA/PyPSA-Eur** (DCU/Insight already use PyPSA for Ireland). Use OATS for operational curtailment/constraint; use PyPSA for "what network/storage build reduces dispatch-down."
5. **Pull ground-truth from EirGrid's System and Renewable Data Reports + Smart Grid Dashboard + SEMO Weekly Operational Constraints Report** for validation, exactly as the arXiv paper does.

**High-value, evidence-backed intervention ideas to explore:**
6. **Co-locate/charge storage in high-SNSP, high-constraint windows** (batteries as load lower SNSP and can absorb constrained energy in the North-West/West/South-West).
7. **Quantify the value of DLR** on the specific North-West/West circuits that define the busiest constraint groups (EirGrid already partners with Heimdall Power on the Lisheen connection, targeting ~30% capacity uplift — a credible, live lever).
8. **Test locational signals / congestion products** — the SEM's single zonal price sends no locational signal; a stylised nodal or zonal-split experiment on the Irish case is a strong theoretical-physics-flavoured contribution.

**Benchmarks that should change your approach:**
- If EirGrid formally raises SNSP to 80% (watch the Operational Policy Roadmap updates), re-weight toward MUON and constraints — noting SNSP's share of dispatch-down already rose from ~1% (2024) to ~17% (2025).
- If/when the North–South Interconnector (target October 2031) or Celtic (target Q4 2028) firm up, NI constraint economics change materially — build scenarios both with and without them.

## Caveats
- **Constraint-group count (~39) and names are from the 1 February 2024 edition** and are explicitly revised over time (groups are added/retired; several are "outage-driven"). Re-check the latest edition on the EirGrid site before quoting a hard number.
- **The named-farm-to-constraint-group mapping is not publicly published** (confirmed gap); only station/node-level membership rules are public.
- **2025 and H1-2026 dispatch-down percentages** are from Wind Energy Ireland / Green Collective / climatejargonbuster.ie analyses of EirGrid data (consistent with EirGrid, but the definitive 2025 figures will be in the *Annual Renewable Energy Constraint and Curtailment Report 2025*, expected ~April 2026 — confirm against it).
- **The "€567m/€700m" cost figures are Network Imperfections Charges** (a broader basket than pure curtailment/constraint) and the 2025/26 number is a contested forecast (SEM-25-053: €883m proposed, €93m disallowed). Do not present them as pure "curtailment cost."
- **arXiv:2302.07143's "80%" applies to curtailment reason codes (2020–21), not total dispatch-down.** Keep the curtailment/constraint distinction explicit throughout.
- **EMS/SCADA vendor detail, exact substation/circuit counts, and Moyle's precise rating** were not pinned to a single primary source here — take live/edition-specific values from the Transmission Forecast Statement or Smart Grid Dashboard.
- **The arXiv:2608.24464 author list** is Nayer, Hodges & Bukhsh (Strathclyde) + Chitambo & Wijeratne (RES) — verify affiliations if citing formally.
- **Braess's-paradox-for-Ireland is not publicly documented** in sources located here; treat as an open, unverified angle.
